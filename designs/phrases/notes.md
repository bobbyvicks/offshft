# Phrase ideas (29 Oct 2026)

Written when the owner asked what else to add after their four phrases (Time Well Earned., The Rest Is Ours., Ours to Spend., 5:00). Three writers, a strict editor, then a quick trademark screen from search results (not legal advice; check USPTO and CIPO before ordering samples).

## The shortlist

Here's what I'd add to go with your four: six more short, plain lines about your time, split between hats and tops. The trademark notes come from quick web searches, so check each one on USPTO and CIPO before ordering samples.

**NOWHERE TO BE.** No shift, no schedule, nobody waiting on you.
- Where: hoodie or crewneck, left chest, stacked NOWHERE / TO BE.
- Trademark: small risk. There's one 2012 US record for the phrase (serial 85784193), and it looks like a dead clerical entry. Look it up on USPTO first.

**SUN’S STILL UP.** You're done work and there's still daylight left.
- Where: rope cap or 5-panel, front, stacked SUN’S / STILL UP. It's a summer line, Victoria Day to Labour Day.
- Trademark: looks clear. The nearest filing is SUN'S OUT, BUNS OUT (US Reg. 4544740). Keep the apostrophe, or it reads like a Phoenix Suns score.

**NO ALARM SET.** Tomorrow's yours, so no alarm tonight.
- Where: crewneck, big centre front on one line, in the Punched Out. spot. The hoodie front works too.
- Trademark: looks clear. The nearest filing is NO ALARM (US serial 73382366), which was abandoned.

**BANKED TIME.** The overtime you saved up, taken as days off.
- Where: dad hat, front, one line. Stacked, it also fits the quarter-zip's left chest.
- Trademark: looks clear. The nearest filing is TIME BANK (US serial 87358423), which is for financial services, not clothing.

**NOT PICKING UP.** Off means off: you're not answering the phone and not taking the extra shift.
- Where: hoodie, big centre front on one line.
- Trademark: looks clear. The nearest filing is an abandoned DONT PICK UP NO MATTER WHAT! (US serial 86227812). It comes from a 12-step saying, so a few people may read the line that way.

**PARKED.** The truck's parked for the night, and so are you.
- Where: trucker, front, one line.
- Trademark: small risk. There's no PARKED filing for clothing. The closest thing is the Disc Golf Pro Tour's Nationally Parked hats (no filing found). It's also one letter off PARKE, a women's clothing brand that sells hats, so look at a mockup.

Didn't make it: BOOTS OFF., because it's a Jon Pardi single from this year and would look like his merch. ABOUT TIME., because it's too common to own and would be a third TIME line next to Time Well Earned. and BANKED TIME. LAKE BY 6., because it's a second summer hat line next to SUN’S STILL UP., and there's a US application for LAKE that covers loungewear.

## The editor's nine, with notes

- **NOWHERE TO BE.** No shift, no schedule, nobody waiting on you.
  - Where: Left chest of a hoodie or crewneck, stacked NOWHERE / TO BE. It's about 65 mm wide at the 6.6 mm minimum cap, the same width as the Punched Out. chest stack. It can also go on the 1469 sweatpants leg once Printful confirms it can embroider there. On one line it's 110 mm, too wide for a quiet hat front.
  - Why: It sounds like a person talking and works on any day off, a Tuesday as much as a Saturday. It's also already in the store's voice: the sweatpants copy says 'Nowhere to be and all day to get there.'
  - Risks: Sia has a 2024 album track called Nowhere to Be, but it isn't a famous line. Out of context it can sound aimless. If it becomes a design, give the sweatpants listings a different line so their copy doesn't repeat another product. A quick web search found no clothing brand using the phrase. One company's NOWHERE mark covers hats and caps (search snippet only), so never shorten this to NOWHERE. USPTO, CIPO and Etsy weren't checked.
- **BOOTS OFF.** Work boots off at the door: the day's done and you're home.
  - Where: Cuffed beanie (toque), one line on the cuff, about 76 mm wide at 6.6 mm caps. Printful's knit area is 5 x 1.75 in. It also fits on one line on a hat front, smaller than Punched Out. (95 mm), or stacked BOOTS / OFF. (44 mm) on a left chest.
  - Why: Two plain words for getting home. Trades know the work boots, every Canadian takes their boots off at the door, and the Q4 toques get a line of their own.
  - Risks: Nurses and office folks don't wear work boots, though winter boots still make the picture work for them. It could pass for a western-wear line. It repeats OFF, so keep it off any piece where the OFF SHFT logo sits right beside it. The S needs the usual terminal trims. A quick web search found no brand or product with the phrase. Etsy, USPTO and CIPO weren't checked.
- **SUN’S STILL UP.** Done work with daylight left to use.
  - Where: Rope cap (the golf hat) or 5-panel front, stacked SUN’S / STILL UP., about 59 mm wide at 6.6 mm caps. On one line it's 101.5 mm, wider than Punched Out., so stack it on hats. It's a summer line, for Victoria Day to Labour Day.
  - Why: It's the best part of a summer day shift, said the way people say it: you're done and there's light left for a line in the water or a quick nine. It needs no icon.
  - Risks: It's only true for part of the year: in a Canadian December it's dark before 5, and it means nothing to night shift, so sell it in summer. The apostrophe is one more small stitch and must be the curly ’. The S's need the usual trims. A quick web search found no song, brand or product with the phrase.
- **NO ALARM SET.** Tomorrow's yours, so there's no alarm tonight.
  - Where: Big centre front of the crewneck on one line, in the large-front spot Punched Out. uses: about 230 mm wide, so about 15 mm caps. The hoodie front works too. Keep it off hats: it's 100 mm on one line at 6.6 mm caps, and stacking it leaves SET. alone on the second line.
  - Why: Anyone who gets up at 4:30 for work knows the night before a day off is the best night of the week, whatever day it lands on.
  - Risks: It faintly echoes Radiohead's 'no alarms and no surprises', but the words are different. Someone might read it as a house alarm left off. It only works on garments. A quick web search found no product with the phrase. Etsy, USPTO and CIPO weren't checked.
- **BANKED TIME.** Overtime saved up and now taken as time off.
  - Where: Dad hat or trucker front, on one line (about 92 mm at 6.6 mm caps, just under Punched Out.) or stacked BANKED / TIME. (53 mm). The stack also suits the quarter-zip's left chest.
  - Why: It's earned time off in the words people use at work: trades, plant workers and nurses bank overtime and take it later as days off.
  - Risks: It's mostly Canadian: Americans tend to say comp time, and to outsiders 'banked' reads as money. It shares the time-as-money idea with Ours to Spend. and the word TIME with Time Well Earned. Sell it alongside them, never on the same piece. A quick web search found no clothing that uses it. USPTO and CIPO weren't checked.
- **NOT PICKING UP.** Off means off: not answering the phone, and not taking the extra shift.
  - Where: Big centre front of the hoodie on one line: about 230 mm wide, so about 14 mm caps. Keep it off hats: it's 107 mm on one line at 6.6 mm caps, and the stack (NOT / PICKING UP.) is lopsided.
  - Why: It's a bit funny without trying. Nurses and plant workers get asked to pick up shifts, and everyone gets the phone call on a day off. It protects the time off without knocking the job.
  - Risks: Out of context it can read as rude, or as refusing to tidy up. Nurse-humour shirts about turning down extra shifts are probably common on Etsy (not checked). A quick web search found no product with the exact phrase. USPTO and CIPO weren't checked.
- **LAKE BY 6.** Off at 5, at the water by 6.
  - Where: Trucker or bucket hat front, on one line: about 70 mm at 6.6 mm caps, close to the logo's 63 mm. It's a summer line.
  - Why: It reads like a text to a buddy: the after-work plan for the cottage and fishing crowd. It names no day of the week and avoids the 'lake life' cliche.
  - Risks: It assumes a lake nearby. In Ontario it can bring Friday cottage traffic to mind, which pulls it toward the weekend. Don't add other 'place at a time' lines like Bags at 5. or Back at 7., or it turns into a formula. A quick web search found no product with the phrase.
- **ABOUT TIME.** Finally: the hours are done and the rest of the day is yours.
  - Where: Dad hat or 5-panel front, on one line: about 83 mm at 6.6 mm caps, a little smaller than Punched Out. Stacked ABOUT / TIME. (44 mm), it goes on a left chest. It also works as a retirement-gift hat.
  - Why: It's what people actually say when the shift ends, the day off comes or a coworker finally retires, and it's two words that fit a hat front on one line.
  - Risks: It's a very common phrase, so nobody can own it, and a 2013 film has the same title. It can sound impatient ('about time you showed up'). With Time Well Earned. and Banked Time., TIME would be on three designs, so pick only one of the two new ones if that's too many. A quick web search found no ABOUT TIME clothing brand or filing, but that isn't a USPTO or CIPO check.
- **PARKED.** The truck's parked for the night, and so are you.
  - Where: Trucker front, one line: about 55 mm at 6.6 mm caps, smaller than the logo. For the couch meaning, the left chest of a hoodie.
  - Why: Drivers are part of the brand's audience but had no line of their own. The same word also covers you, parked on the couch.
  - Risks: It's the same kind of one-word 'done' as Punched Out., so keep the two off the same piece. On its own it can look unfinished ('parked where?'), and 'parked' can also mean shelved. A quick web search found no PARKED clothing brand. USPTO and CIPO weren't checked.
