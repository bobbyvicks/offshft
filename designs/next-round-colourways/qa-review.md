The artificial fabric texture is a real limitation, especially in several dark/mid-tone mockups; it should not be treated as an accurate fabric finish.

## Concrete limitations

- **Most visible texture issue: 12 Pepper.** Repeating curved, embossed-looking marks cover the back and sleeves. They remain noticeable in the full garment view and are more distracting than ordinary fine grain.
- **Next most visible: 14 Pepper, 14 Blue Jean and 11 Blue Jean.** Similar small, winding marks across broad fabric areas. They reduce photographic realism, but the artwork and colour combinations stay clear. 11 Pepper and 14 Espresso also show this effect at closer inspection.
- The three caps preserve coherent mesh, seams and brim geometry. Their texture is more plausibly integrated with the twill and is less distracting than the worst sweatshirt/tee frames.
- All hoodie variants correctly show only the back; the hood clears the lettering. All garments are fully framed without missing cuffs, cropped graphics or merged extra parts.
- The narrow horizontal opening on the left of the **5** also appears in `../14-five-oclock/design-light.png`. It belongs to the source letterform and is not a stray generation streak; the earlier v2 concern about that detail is withdrawn.

No regeneration is necessary for choosing colourways from this draft set. If these images become large standalone product pictures, replace the worst texture cases first; the current images should remain labelled as concepts. SVG artwork remains authoritative for exact letterforms, colours and dimensions.
